Scalded

Mortadella and scalded products

The meat emulsion is the trade’s technical exam. Either the protein encapsulates the fat, or the fat separates and the whole product is lost. And it is all decided in the degrees the cutter shows.

Formulation per 100 kg of batter

ComponentAmountWhy it is there
Lean beef35 – 40 kgBeef protein emulsifies better than pork: it is the backbone of the product.
Pork15 – 20 kgFlavour and lighter colour.
Back fat18 – 22 kgBack fat is firm and emulsifies; soft jowl breaks down sooner.
Flake ice15 – 20 kgIt is an ingredient, not a trick: it provides the water of the emulsion and controls temperature.
Common salt1.8 – 2.0 kgSolubilises myosin. Without salt there is no possible emulsion.
Curing salt (6.25 % nitrite)0.25 kgEquivalent to ~156 ppm ingoing nitrite. See the dosage sheet.
Phosphates0.3 – 0.5 kgRaise pH and water-holding capacity. Almost every country caps this at 5 g/kg.
Sodium erythorbate or ascorbate0.05 kgSpeeds up curing and protects colour. 500 ppm is the working dose.
Soy protein or caseinate1 – 3 kgStabilises the emulsion and adjusts cost.
Starch2 – 4 kgHolds water during scalding. Too much gives a rubbery texture.
Seasoning and pepper0.4 – 0.6 kg
Process

Step by step, with the temperatures.

01

Cutter, first stage

Lean meat + salt + phosphate + one third of the ice. Chop to 4 – 6 °C. This is where the protein is extracted: the step that decides everything.

02

Cutter, second stage

Add fat and the rest of the ice. Chop at high speed until the batter is glossy and homogeneous.

03

Temperature ceiling

Do not exceed 12 °C in a fine batter, 14 °C as absolute limit. Above that the fat melts and the emulsion breaks with no way back.

04

Stuff

Large calibre casing, fibrous or plastic. No air.

05

Scald

Water or steam at 75 – 80 °C until the geometric centre reaches 70 – 72 °C. Measured with a probe, not with a clock.

06

Thermal shock

Immediate cold shower down below 30 °C, then into the chiller. Stops cooking and prevents wrinkling.

Troubleshooting

What went wrong, and why.

Half the craft is reading the defect. These are the ones that keep coming back.

What you seeWhat caused it
Fat ring under the casingThe emulsion broke: batter above 14 °C, or fat added before the protein was extracted.
Rubbery productToo much starch, or too much water for the protein available.
Gelatin pocketsScalding too aggressive, or badly chopped collagen that melted out.
Grey on the cut faceInsufficient or badly distributed nitrite, or no erythorbate. Oxygen in the pack does it too.
Frequently asked

What people ask before they start.

What do phosphates actually do?

They raise the pH of the meat away from the proteins’ isoelectric point, and with that the meat holds more water. That is what lets you add ice without the product releasing it during scalding. Most regulations cap them at 5 g/kg expressed as P₂O₅ on the finished product.

Why 72 °C and not higher?

At 70 – 72 °C in the core you achieve the required lethality for vegetative pathogens and the protein is fully coagulated. Going beyond adds no safety and costs yield: the product releases water and shrinks.

Can it be made without a cutter?

At small scale you substitute a fine-plate grinder plus a mixer, accepting a grainier texture. What cannot be substituted is temperature control.
